# Draconia's Redemption

### At the Battle of Hogwarts

#### Part 2: The Storm

---

Bellatrix Lestrange fell, and the Great Hall held its breath.

She lay where Molly Weasley's curse had dropped her, her heavy black hair spread across the stone, her eyes wide with a surprise that would never leave them now.

Voldemort's scream tore through the silence. McGonagall, Kingsley and Slughorn, who had been fighting him, were blasted away like ragdolls. Harry, invisible beneath the Cloak, watched him raise his wand on the small, red-haired witch who had just taken the life of his most faithful servant. He rushed forward and lifted his wand. The Shield Charm was on his lips—

—and something in the Hall changed.

Harry felt it before he saw it. A pressure in his ears, every hair on his arms rising under the Cloak. All around him some people froze in the middle of their duels, as though they had heard their names called.

Voldemort felt it too. His wand halted, still pointed at Molly, and for the first time that night the Dark Lord hesitated. He looked up.

High above, the enchanted ceiling had been brightening with the approaching dawn. Now, all at once, it went dark. Clouds poured down from nowhere, black and boiling, as if a bucket of ink had been spilled on the firmament. A wind rose in the Great Hall, and rain began to fall, out of a ceiling that had only ever pretended to be the sky. Someone had taken hold of it.

Voldemort turned away from Molly Weasley, seeking the source.

So did Harry.

All around him chaos reigned in the Great Hall. Many people still running, ducking, duelling, falling, spells flying in every direction. And through the middle of it walked a single figure.

Draconia Malfoy.

She moved in a perfectly straight line down the center, with clear purpose, ignoring the chaos. A curse flew past her face, and she did not flinch. Students and Death Eaters parted instinctively to get out of her path.

Her face was streaked with soot, dust and tears. Her eyes swollen, red and blazing. Teeth exposed and clenched tight. She was not looking at Molly. She was not looking at her dead aunt on the floor. Her gaze was locked onto Voldemort as though there were nobody else in the world. As though there were no world left at all.

She raised her wand and screamed, in fury and in grief, raw and wordless. It sounded like something that had been locked away for a very long time, and had just broken its chains.

And the sky answered her.

Up in the black clouds, the lightning began to move. It gathered. Bolt after bolt crawled together across the dark, white and branching, finding one another, joining, until they wove together into a single, terrifying shape up there in the storm. A long, narrow head. A neck that curled like smoke. Wings that stretched from one wall of the Hall to the other.

A dragon.

Harry had seen dragons of fire in the Room of Requirement earlier that night, and he had flown through them with Draconia holding his waist. This was different. This creature was not warm. It was made of storm. Lightning sparked through it like living veins, its eyes were two blinding flashes of white, and when it opened its jaws, the sound that roared was thunder itself.

Voldemort swung the Elder Wand, summoning a shield of shimmering silver that sprang up before him. He poured every ounce of power into the barrier.

The dragon fell on him.

It came down in one white arc, the full height of the Great Hall. It hit the shield the way the sea hits a cliff, and the spray of energy splashed the Hall.

The beast exploded against the shield, shattering into a hundred shards of lightning. They raced along the walls and across the floor and up into the clouds and down again, striking and leaping and striking again, as though the Great Hall were a jar and the storm had been shut inside it. The tall windows shattered, one after another, all down the length of the Hall. The ancient stone walls blackened and cracked where the bolts hit.

Nobody could fight in that. Nobody tried. The whole Hall went down, Death Eaters and defenders of Hogwarts alike, flattened like grass under a gust, arms wrapped over their heads. Harry was on his knees on the wet stone with no memory of falling, clutching the Cloak shut with both fists. Draconia's scream and the lightning filled the Hall, and there was no room left in it for anything else.

It was not a spell. It was older than spells: a primal magic, unleashed by primal emotions.

For one heartbeat Voldemort's shield held. Harry saw it bend; he saw fractures spreading across the surface, like ice breaking under a boot.  The force lifted the Dark Lord off his feet and threw him down onto his back on the floor of the Great Hall.

The lightning began to circle. It caught two Death Eaters rushing to aid their master and swept them away like dry leaves. They lay senseless in the puddles. The storm formed a crackling ring of light in the center of the Hall, a cage with only two inmates — the girl standing firm, and the Dark Lord on the floor, fighting for his life. Bolt after bolt crashed against his shield as he lay there, and he held it up with the Elder Wand gripped in both hands.

Nobody was fighting anymore. Attackers and defenders lay side by side in the rain, staring up at the same terrifying spectacle. For that moment they were not two armies. They were only spectators, watching something none of them believed was possible.

Harry had seen Voldemort forced to defend himself once before, in the Atrium of the Ministry of Magic. The wizard fighting him then had been Albus Dumbledore. Now Voldemort lay on his back in the rain with his red eyes wide, and for one moment he looked exactly like a man who had not been afraid of anything for fifty years, had forgotten how it felt, and now had to remember.

Harry could not move. He could only watch.

Molly Weasley was staring at the girl, and Harry saw the shock on her face turn slowly into something else. Understanding. Only one thing could tear that kind of power out of a witch. Molly had found it in herself just a minute ago. But Molly had never had to hide hers. This had been hidden for years, chained, held down and called by every name but its own, and now it was loose, all of it, at once.

Ron was on his knees, staring with his mouth hanging open. Beside him, Hermione had both hands pressed over her mouth, crying, but not just for Harry now.

Professor McGonagall was rising up slowly. Her lips were pressed into a thin, hard line, she was witnessing a transmutation of the highest order, way above the physical plane that she taught. For the first time in her life, she wanted to shout *"A thousand points to Slytherin!"* If only the tragedy wasn't so absolute.

Lucius Malfoy stood with his empty hands half-raised, staring at his daughter as though he had never seen her before. And perhaps he never had.

And Narcissa was not looking at her daughter at all. She was looking around the ruined Hall, quickly, desperately, from face to face, as though she were searching for someone.

Harry knew who she was searching for.

Draconia had moved forward while the lightning raged, step by step, her wand pointed at the man on the floor, and the entire storm seemed to be pouring out of her through that one thin arm.

And it was costing her.

Harry saw it before he understood it. Blood trickled from her ear and nose, over her lip and down her chin, mixing with the tears.

Then the lightning began to come apart.

And she was coming apart with it. Her eyes began to lose focus. Electric sparks were branching from her wrist, towards her arm and face, peeling her sleeves and scarring her skin. Her whole body was shaking. She took a step forward and her knee gave way. She dragged herself up again and screamed. Her wand cracked.

Voldemort got up.

He did it slowly and carefully, like a man rising from ice. He did not strike back, not yet. He held the Elder Wand before him, the shield still glowing in front of it, and stood behind it and watched her; and Harry, watching him, saw the shock leave that white face and something colder take its place.

Patience.

Voldemort simply waited, as one waits for a fire to burn itself out.

And it did.

With a flick of his wrist — small, almost lazy — the spell cut through the last of her storm like a knife through butter. It caught her cracked wand first snapping it. The blast behind it hurled her backwards. She hit the floor and slid across the wet stone.

The last of the lightning died out. Above them, the clouds came apart and drained away, and the ceiling was only a ceiling again, grey with the coming morning. The rain thinned, and stopped.

Draconia tried to push herself up on her elbows, but her limbs gave way.

"Draconia," Voldemort said. His cold voice reached every corner of the Hall. "Lucius's daughter."

She coughed blood. Last of the strength deserted her. She lay motionless, defeated, barely conscious, and yet, Harry could see no fear in her tired eyes. She was prepared to die. And she didn't care.

Voldemort tilted his head, studying her.

"Last year I gave you one old man to kill, and your hand shook. And now…" He spread his arms, revealing the devastation around them. "…all this."

Voldemort walked towards her across the wet floor, unhurried, stood over her, and looked down.

"Love," he said softly, tasting the word as if it were poison. "How very disappointing."

Harry heard it. Everyone heard it.

A memory surfaced in Harry's mind. The Mirror of Erised, and her voice saying all she could see in it was the two of them. He had thought she was mocking him.

He remembered a bathroom floor a year ago, her blood in the water, and her whisper asking whether he hated her that much. He had thought it was the pain talking.

He remembered her arms around him tonight, on the broom, while escaping the fire, holding on so tightly he could hardly breathe. He had thought it was only fear.

An hour ago, in Snape's memories, he had watched a fifteen-year-old girl insist that she felt nothing at all, and he had convinced himself it was long ago, fake and over.

So on the grounds, where Hargid laid his body, when he watched her fall to her knees, he didn’t think anything. He knew. It was not over.

It had been growing all this time in the dark, the way roots grow beneath stone, and now the stone had broken.

Voldemort raised the Elder Wand.

"Save her!"

It was Narcissa Malfoy's voice, high and cracked with terror. She wasn't screaming at the Dark Lord. She was screaming at the Hall, at the empty air, at someone nobody else knew was there.

And Harry, who had not been able to move since the sky went dark, knew who she was screaming to, and found that he could.

"*Protego!*"

The Shield Charm burst open in the dead center of the Great Hall, a barrier of light forming between the Elder Wand and the girl on the floor, just as the jet of green light erupted from Voldemort's wand. The curse hit the barrier and vanished. Voldemort's head snapped around, searching for the source of the magic. Harry pulled off the Cloak.

For one heartbeat the whole Hall was utterly silent.

"Still disappointing?" said Harry.

The Hall erupted.

"Harry!"

"HE'S ALIVE!"

Screams, shouts from a hundred mouths at once. And behind him, from the floor, under all of them, so quietly that perhaps only he heard it, came one more voice.

"Harry," she whispered.

And in his head it rang louder than every scream in the Hall.

In seven years she had never once said it. All that time, she must have been keeping it somewhere, like a letter she could never send. And she had sent it now, from the floor, with blood on her lips.

He could hear her ragged breathing behind him, and it was the best sound he had heard all night. He did not turn around. He kept his eyes locked on Voldemort. If he looked at her now, he would not be able to do what came next.

"I don't want anyone else to try to help," he said loudly, and in the silence his voice carried to every corner of the Hall. "It's got to be like this. It's got to be me."

He spoke it to the crowd.

He meant her.
